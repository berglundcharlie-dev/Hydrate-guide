01 / Understand the basics

Why hydration matters

Water helps regulate body temperature, transport nutrients, support circulation, and maintain normal cell function. During exercise, you lose water and electrolytes through sweat.

Exercise→Sweat loss→Fluid + sodium

Sweat rates and sodium losses vary widely between athletes and conditions.

What is happening?

💧 Fluid

Supports blood volume and helps the body release heat through sweating.

⚡ Electrolytes

Sodium is the main electrolyte lost in sweat. It helps regulate fluid balance and supports nerve and muscle function.

🌡️ Temperature

Hot or humid conditions can increase heat strain and make hydration planning more important.

02 / Choose what fits

Water vs. sports drinks

Neither option is automatically best in every situation. Match the drink to the demands of the activity.

EVERYDAY DEFAULT

💧 Water

Usually the best choice for daily hydration and many shorter or less demanding workouts.

  • No added sugar or calories
  • Works well when meals provide the electrolytes you need
  • Easy to access and carry
  • A good option before, during, and after many routine sessions
SITUATION-DEPENDENT

⚡ Sports drinks

Can be useful when exercise is prolonged or intense, sweat losses are high, or another demanding session is coming soon.

  • Provide fluid and typically sodium
  • Many contain carbohydrate for energy during longer activity
  • May help replace some sweat losses
  • Often unnecessary for casual activity or short workouts

Remember: duration is only one factor. Intensity, heat, humidity, individual sweat rate, access to fluids, and the next training session also matter.

03 / Put it into practice

A simple hydration game plan

Think ahead, respond to the conditions, and replace fluids sensibly. Avoid forcing large amounts of water.

Before exercise

Start prepared

Drink fluids regularly with meals and throughout the day. Begin exercise feeling normally hydrated rather than trying to catch up at the last minute.

During exercise

Match the session

For many shorter sessions, water is enough. For prolonged, intense, hot, or high-sweat sessions, a sports drink may be useful. Use breaks and available fluids sensibly.

After exercise

Recover gradually

Drink with your recovery meal or snack. Include normal foods that provide fluids, sodium, and carbohydrates. If you have another session soon, pay closer attention to replacing losses.

⚠️ Avoid overdrinking

Drinking more fluid than your body can get rid of during exercise can dangerously dilute blood sodium (exercise-associated hyponatremia). Do not force fluids or follow a one-size-fits-all volume target. A sports dietitian or athletic trainer can help create an individualized plan.

04 / Hydration through food

Food contributes, too

Fluids come from beverages and foods. A balanced meal or snack can also help replace energy and electrolytes after training.

Example post-practice options

Greek yogurt with fruit and cereal • A turkey sandwich with fruit • Rice, chicken, and vegetables • Milk or a fortified alternative with a snack. Choose foods that fit your preferences, schedule, and overall nutrition needs.
